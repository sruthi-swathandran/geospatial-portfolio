Each marker is one test chip, about 1.5 km across, coloured by the share of its hand-labelled parcels a method recovered. Pick a method above the map, and zoom in to a chip to see its labelled parcels, filled where the method found them and outlined in orange where it missed. In India the released checkpoint leaves almost every chip empty, while the same imagery gives other methods something to find. In Slovenia, where the checkpoint was trained on a complete cadastre, it works.

How to read it

Indian chips carry about five hand-drawn parcels each and nothing else is labelled, so a chip's colour is the share of those five a method recovered. Slovenian chips carry a complete cadastre, about 37 parcels each.

Dashed rings are test chips with no labelled parcel, mostly forest in Slovenia.

Settings

Each method is shown at the one setting whose objects per chip sit nearest the checkpoint's own count, so the methods spend a similar budget. The headline figures in the project write-up are interpolated to the exact count and differ a little from the totals here.

What it does not show

Chip outcomes are a handful of parcels each, so a single chip is a weak signal. Read the pattern, and the district totals, rather than any one marker. The causes of the gap between the countries are discussed in the full write-up.
