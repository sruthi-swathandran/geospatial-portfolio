How the extent was made

Water is dark to radar because a calm surface reflects the pulse away from the sensor. A single Sentinel-1 pass on 12 August 2016 was speckle filtered with a 5×5 Lee window and thresholded at −18.75 dB on VH, an operating point chosen against the hand-labelled Sen1Floods11 chips for this event.

Change detection against an earlier date was tested and rejected. The best change threshold was the one that made the constraint inert, and the change signal alone scored 0.147 IoU against 0.130 for labelling the entire scene as water.

What the numbers depend on

Three things are dark to radar without being wet, and each was found by looking at the map rather than by any metric. A 600 m buffer inside the swath boundary is marked no data, where flood classification ran at 17% of area against 1.9% in the interior. Ground steeper than 8° becomes land, since 99.3% of the permanent channel sits below that slope. Components under 10 pixels are dropped. The chain runs 217,490 → 191,267 → 122,830 → 119,779 ha.

None of those three steps is validated. The hand labels cover 65 floodplain chips and none of these situations appears in them. Each is justified by a measurement on the scene rather than against ground truth.

Permanent water means JRC Global Surface Water occurrence of 50% or more. Moving that definition between 10% and 90% moves flood extent between 11,067 and 23,423 ha on the validation chips. The definitions move the answer more than the method does.

Which number, and how sure

The threshold was chosen twice against two defensible criteria. At −18.75 dB the map agrees best with hand labels pixel by pixel; at −17.75 dB its total area is unbiased against them. The two score 0.519 and 0.522 IoU, indistinguishable inside a confidence interval of ±0.11, and they differ by 42% on flood area and 86% on flooded cropland. This map draws the first; the range beside each figure gives the second.

Ranking survives the choice even though magnitude does not. Nagaon, Morigaon and Sonitpur are first, second and third at both thresholds.

Accuracy on the 65 labelled chips is IoU 0.519, 95% interval 0.395 to 0.609, bootstrapped over chips. Macro IoU is 0.280 with a minimum of zero, so the method fails outright on some chips. Nothing here has been checked against an independent flood map: no contemporaneous district-wise inundation figure for this event was retrievable from open archives.

Read this before quoting a figure

Cropland comes from ESA WorldCover 2021, four to five years after the event, because no open 10 m cropland map exists for India in 2016. Land that changed use in between is misattributed.

District boundaries are present-day geoBoundaries ADM2, and Assam has created districts since 2016, so these will not match a 2016 bulletin one for one. Percentages are of the cropland imaged, and rows where the swath caught only part of a district are flagged.

The map layer here is a 200 m preview render of a 20 m raster. 9,698 ha of flood fell outside Indian district polygons, in Bangladesh, Bhutan and the Arunachal border strip. Districts the swath edge crosses lose real water to the buffer as well as artefact: Majuli is the clearest case.
